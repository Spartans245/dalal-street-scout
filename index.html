CONCEPT 1 · VPB — VOLUME-PRICE BREAKOUT
COILING
Stage 1 of VPB · Watchlist only
WHAT
Price swings get smaller each day — the stock is coiling like a spring. Volume is also shrinking, meaning fewer sellers are willing to sell at lower prices.
WHY IT MATTERS
Low volume = sellers are exhausting. When supply dries up like this, even a small increase in buying demand sends the price sharply higher. Energy is building. Set an alert — don't buy yet.
BREAKOUT
Stage 2 of VPB · Small position
WHAT
After days of coiling, one strong candle breaks out with volume surging above average. The candle closes in the top 60%+ of its range — buyers controlled the day completely.
WHY IT MATTERS
Volume is the lie detector. A breakout on high volume = real demand, not a trap. The spring released. Buy a small position today. If next day confirms, add more.
HOW VPB IS CALCULATED — SETUP + TRIGGER
SETUP — checked over last 5 days
price_coiling — high/low range < 4% over last 5 days. Energy compressing like a spring.
vol_shrinking — volume < 85% of 20d avg for last 3 days. Sellers are exhausting.
TRIGGER — measured on today's candle
vol_ratio = today's volume ÷ 20d avg volume. How much bigger than normal?
close_pos = (close − low) ÷ (high − low). Did buyers hold the gains? 1.0 = closed at high.

CONCEPT 2 · EMA CROSS SIGNALS (14 EMA × 50 EMA)
The 14-day EMA (fast) crossing above the 50-day EMA (slow) is a trend change confirmation. Three entry opportunities exist around this event.
PRE-CROSS
Partial entry
WHAT: Two conditions must both be true simultaneously: (1) EMA14 is below EMA50 but gap < 0.5% of price AND EMA14 is rising fast — cross is 1 day away. (2) A VPB breakout or weak_breakout has fired on the same candle.
WHY VPB IS REQUIRED HERE: EMA convergence alone can be slow and deceptive — two EMAs can drift toward each other for weeks without anything happening. The VPB breakout is the confirmation of real buying pressure happening right now, on this specific candle. Without VPB firing, the stock stays in TRENDING or COILING. When both signals align on the same day, you have a stock that is coiled, breaking out, AND about to cross — that's a very high-probability setup.
WHY ENTER BEFORE THE CROSS: The cross itself is a lagging event — it is calculated from averages of past prices. By the time EMA14 officially crosses EMA50, most of the initial move has already happened. Entering at PRE-CROSS (when VPB fires and gap < 0.5%) means buying when the fuel is loading, not after the rocket has launched. Lower price, tighter stop, better R/R.

Full position
WHAT: EMA14 crossed above EMA50 within the last 5 days. No VPB required. What matters instead is volume on the cross day itself: ≥ 1.5× 20-day average = vol-confirmed cross (+18 pts). Score decays with recency: 1–2d ago (+18/14), 3–4d (+14/10), 5d (+10/8).

WHY VOLUME ON CROSS DAY MATTERS: A cross on low volume can be a "fake cross" — just price noise moving averages slightly. A cross with volume ≥ 1.5× average means real institutional buying drove the price through the 50 EMA. That's the difference between a signal and a trap.

PULLBACK
Re-entry / kiss-back
WHAT

WHY PULLBACK > POST-CROSS ENTRY
▸ Lower entry price — you're buying after the initial surge settles
▸ EMA14 acts as dynamic support — natural hard stop just below it
▸ Better risk/reward — tighter stop loss, more room to target
▸ Cross is confirmed — survived the pullback, not a fake cross

The KISS bonus (+3 in ALL tab) rewards the better entry price, but the underlying signal quality comes from the original cross event. A pullback after a vol-confirmed cross is far more reliable than one after a thin-volume cross.

TARGET SELECTION LOGIC

⚠ MM is only computed for BREAKOUT, PRE-CROSS and COILING stages. For POST-CROSS, PULLBACK, TRENDING and NONE — MM is excluded and the target falls back to 52W High or ATH(9M).

MM — MEASURED MOVE
MM Target = 5-day HIGH + (HIGH − LOW)
WORKED EXAMPLE
5d High = ₹542  ·  5d Low = ₹460
Range height H = 542 − 460 = ₹82
MM Target = 542 + 82 = ₹624
Breakout close = ₹548  →  Max Upside = +13.9%
Markets tend to travel at least as far from the breakout as the range that compressed before it. This is the minimum expected move.
VALID STAGES ONLY
MM is shown as the target only for BREAKOUT and PRE-CROSS stocks — where the coiling pattern is intact and a measured move is meaningful.
COILING shows MM with a ! flag — conditional on breakout firing. POST-CROSS, PULLBACK, TRENDING and NONE fall back to 52W High or ATH(9M).
52W — 52-WEEK HIGH
Overhead resistance — official 1-year high from NSE  ·  2,738/2,749 stocks
Traders who bought near the 52W high are sitting on losses. As price approaches, they sell to break even — creating supply that caps the move. Sourced from NSE — more accurate than a rolling Kite window.
ATH(9M) — 9-MONTH HIGH
Used when price has already broken the 52W High
Once a stock breaks above the 52W High, there's no supply from that zone. The 9-month ATH becomes the new resistance ceiling to target.

MCAP COLUMN — MARKET CAPITALISATION (shown in scanner, not scored)
MCap = total shares outstanding × current price. It tells you the size of the company, not just the share price.

Why MCap matters more than price: A ₹500 share of a ₹200 Cr company is a micro-cap. A ₹50 share of a ₹5000 Cr company is a solid mid-cap. Share price alone tells you nothing — MCap tells you the full picture.

MCap is also used as a tiebreaker in sorting: when two stocks have the same score and the same upside, the larger-MCap stock ranks higher (more established, lower risk).
SWING SUPPORT & RESISTANCE — HOW IT WORKS
WHAT IS A SWING HIGH / LOW?
Each bar = 1 trading day (a daily candle: open, high, low, close). A Swing High is a day whose high price is higher than the 5 trading days before it AND higher than the 5 trading days after it — meaning price reached a local peak there and reversed. A Swing Low is the mirror: a day whose low price is lower than the 5 days on each side. These are price memory zones — traders who bought near a swing low remember it as a good entry; traders trapped near a swing high want to exit at breakeven.
The chart below shows 13 daily candles. Bar 7 (marked ★) is the Swing High — its high of ₹320 is the highest of all 5 bars to its left and all 5 bars to its right. That confirms it as a real swing high.
The tall red candle (★) reached ₹320 on that day. All 5 green candles to its left and all 5 red candles to its right have a lower high — confirming it is a Swing High. Swing Low works identically but checks that the day's low is lower than the 5 days on each side. The cluster of buyers/sellers at these price levels creates the support and resistance you see in the modal.
HOW IT IS CALCULATED — STEP BY STEP
1
Scan 193 daily bars (9M Kite history). For each bar i, check if High[i] is the highest among bars i−5 to i+5. If yes → Swing High. Same check for Low[i] as lowest → Swing Low.
2
Collect all swing prices — both highs and lows go into one list and are sorted ascending.
3
Cluster nearby levels — if two swing prices are within 1.5% of each other they belong to the same zone. The zone price = average of all points in the cluster. Strength = how many swing points collapsed into that cluster.
4
Split by current price — clusters > price × 1.005 → Resistance. Clusters < price × 0.995 → Support. Show the 3 nearest on each side.

HOW TO USE SWING S/R IN YOUR DECISION
SUPPORT → ENTRY CONFIRMATION
✅ Strong support nearby (<3% below) — price is sitting on a proven floor. Adds conviction to a BREAKOUT or PRE-CROSS entry.
Example with numbers: Stock at ₹287. Support at ₹280 ●●●○○ (−2.4% below). VPB fires today — breakout candle closes at ₹293.
Your entry is ₹293. If the trade goes wrong, price should hold at ₹280 before it breaks further. So your real downside is ₹293 → ₹280 = −4.4%, not the full −9% stop.
The support gives you a tighter, well-defined risk level. You know exactly where the floor is — if price closes below ₹280, you exit. Without this support, you would not know where to exit until the full −9% stop is hit.
✅ Multiple-touch support (●●●+) — the zone has been tested and held before. More likely to act as a springboard again.
⚠ No support nearby — price is in open air between levels. Wider stop needed. Reduce position size.
RESISTANCE → TARGET & EXIT PLANNING
🎯 Resistance close to Max Upside target — confirms the target is a real ceiling, not just a formula number. High conviction to exit near there.
⚠ Strong resistance between entry and target — price may stall before reaching the Max Upside. Consider partial exit at resistance.
❌ Resistance immediately overhead (<2%) — risk/reward is unfavourable. Skip or wait for the level to break first.

CHAPTER 1
Price Movements
Price is not a fact. It is a continuous negotiation between people who disagree about value. It rises when buyers are more convinced than sellers. It falls when sellers are more convinced than buyers. The same person can be a buyer at ₹80 and a seller at ₹100.
WHY PRICES RISE
SCENARIO 1 — SUPPLY IS ABSORBED · COMPETITION HAPPENED
Both sides showed up at ₹300. Sellers offered, buyers bid — a real contest. Buyers absorbed every share sellers had. Once supply was consumed the sellers lost. The next buyer must pay higher to find a new seller. Price rises because the competition was resolved — buyers won.
Most durable rise. Those sellers competed and lost — their supply is structurally gone. Price does not need to fight them again. High volume is the evidence the contest happened.
SCENARIO 2 — SELLERS DISAPPEAR · NO COMPETITION
No contest at all. Sellers moved their asks higher — they want more money and won't sell at current price. No transaction needed to happen. Buyers didn't have to outbid anyone. Price drifts into empty space. Volume is low because nobody is on the other side. The sellers didn't lose — they simply retreated.
Fragile rise. Sellers didn't lose — they're still there, asking more. They can return at any price they choose and reverse the move instantly. Low volume is the tell.
SCENARIO 3 — DEMAND FROM A PROVEN FLOOR
Price returns to a support level where buyers have stepped in before. Those buyers step in again. Example: Stock at ₹280 (support tested 3 times). Price dips to ₹280. Buyers who anchored here buy again. Price bounces toward ₹310 (next resistance).
A bounce, not a breakout. Price rises back toward the middle of the range. The ceiling (resistance) is still intact. Target = next resistance, not infinity.
SCENARIO 4 — FORCED BUYING (SHORT SQUEEZE)
Short sellers bet price will fall. When price rises and hits their stop, they must buy back — regardless of their view. This buying is not discretionary. Example: 1,000 traders shorted at ₹300. Price breaks ₹305 (their stop). All 1,000 must buy. Their forced buying adds to demand and accelerates the move.
Explains why breakouts through resistance are often explosive — new buyers + forced short covering happening simultaneously.
SCENARIO 5 — NEW INFORMATION RESETS VALUE
Earnings beat, FII buying, sector tailwind. The perceived value of the stock changes. Buyers who thought ₹300 was fair now think ₹420 is fair. All existing resistance levels become temporarily irrelevant. Price can jump through multiple resistance levels at once. A gap-up through resistance signals information reset, not absorption.
SCENARIO 6 — MOMENTUM & FOMO
Price is already rising. Traders who missed the move buy in fear of missing more. Trend followers trigger. Self-reinforcing — rising price attracts more buyers. No structural basis. Continues as long as new buyers keep arriving. The most dangerous scenario to trade late. When the last buyer has bought, there is no one left to sell to.
WHY PRICES FALL — THE PARADOX
The paradox: if price rose from ₹80 to ₹100, doesn't that mean all sellers below ₹100 were already absorbed?
Yes — but "no sellers below ₹100" applied to the sellers who existed at that time. It does not mean new sellers cannot appear. The people who bought on the way up from ₹80 to ₹100 are now sitting on profit. They have become potential sellers. Every buyer creates a future seller.
BUYERS STEP AWAY
The buyers who pushed price to ₹100 wanted to own it at those prices. Once they bought, they stop bidding. Their demand is fulfilled. No new buyers arrive to replace them. The next seller drops their ask from ₹100 to ₹99 to find a buyer. Then ₹98. Price falls not from aggressive selling — simply from buyers walking away.
THE CASCADE
Price dips from ₹100 to ₹97. The trader who bought at ₹95 gets uncomfortable — their trade is nearly at breakeven. They sell at ₹95. That pushes price to ₹93. The trader who bought at ₹92 now sees a loss and sells. Each forced seller creates the next.
WHY SUPPORT SOMETIMES HOLDS, SOMETIMES BREAKS
When price falls back to ₹80 (old support), two forces meet. Coming down: weak buyers who entered late and are cutting losses. Waiting below: original buyers who bought at ₹80 and held the entire move — they know this level is significant and buy more. If these original buyers are still present and committed, support holds. If they too have sold or lost conviction, support breaks to the next level.
THE KEY PRINCIPLE
No level is permanently clear of sellers. Sellers are created continuously — by profit taking, by fear, by forced liquidation (margin calls), by changing information. The only thing that matters at any moment is whether the buyers at that level are more committed than the sellers. Price does not remember where it came from. Only the participants do.
CHAPTER 2
Price & Volume
Price tells you where the market agreed. Volume tells you how much conviction was behind that agreement. A price move with high volume is a decision made by many. A price move with low volume is a decision made by few — and few decisions are easily reversed.
THE CORE PRINCIPLE
PRICE UP + HIGH VOLUME ✓ (CONFIRMED)
Many buyers participated. Sellers were overwhelmed. The move has broad conviction behind it. Example: Stock rises from ₹280 to ₹300. Volume is 3× the 20-day average. Institutions, traders, and retail all buying. This move is likely to sustain because so many people committed capital to it.
PRICE UP + LOW VOLUME ⚠ (SUSPECT)
Price rose but few participants. Sellers stopped offering — price drifted into a vacuum. Not many buyers actually committed capital. Example: Stock drifts from ₹280 to ₹295 over 5 days, volume 40% of average. Now many sellers sit at ₹295 but no buyers match them — no transaction, price freezes. It does not fall yet. The fall comes when one impatient seller hits the best bid below (say ₹288) — that transaction prints at ₹288 and price drops. One blink triggers the collapse.
PRICE DOWN + HIGH VOLUME ✓ (CONFIRMED SELLING)
Many sellers participated. Buyers were overwhelmed. Real distribution happening. Example: Stock falls from ₹300 to ₹275, volume 2.5× average. Institutions are exiting. This is not a dip to buy — it is genuine selling pressure. The fall is likely to continue.
PRICE DOWN + LOW VOLUME ✓ (HEALTHY PULLBACK)
Price falls but sellers are not aggressive. Few participants. This is profit-taking, not distribution. Example: Stock dips from ₹300 to ₹288 on 50% of average volume. Holders are not panicking. Sellers are thin. This is the healthy pullback — the PULLBACK stage. Buyers can step in without fighting large supply.
WHY PRICE SOMETIMES MOVES WITHOUT VOLUME
THIN ORDER BOOK
If only 10 sellers are sitting in the order book from ₹285 to ₹300, a single buyer with modest size can move price ₹15 upward. Not because of massive demand — because supply is thin. Small cap and SME stocks move this way. Large volume is not needed when the order book is sparse.
ALGORITHMIC REPRICING
Market makers and algorithms adjust quotes based on related instruments — index moves, futures, sector ETFs. The stock's price changes even with zero direct volume. No one bought or sold the stock — but the perceived fair value moved because something correlated to it moved.
BID-ASK SPREAD MOVEMENT
A seller cancels their sell order at ₹285. The next available seller is at ₹292. The last traded price was ₹285. No trade has occurred but the market price has "moved" to ₹292 on any quote. Volume is zero. Price moved purely because someone withdrew supply.

CHAPTER 3
Close Position
Every candle tells a story about who controlled the session. Close position is the number that makes that story precise. It is universal — not just for breakout days, but for every single day in a chart.
THE FORMULA
close_pos = (Close − Low) ÷ (High − Low)
It measures where the close sits within the day's own range. 1.0 means price closed at the exact high. 0.0 means it closed at the exact low. 0.5 means it closed dead in the middle.
≥ 0.70
Buyers dominated
Closed near high
0.40 – 0.70
Inconclusive
Neither side dominant
≤ 0.30
Sellers dominated
Closed near low
CRITICAL — CLOSE_POS VS DAY-OVER-DAY PRICE CHANGE ARE INDEPENDENT
A stock can rise from yesterday's close AND have a low close_pos on the same day. They measure completely different things:
DAY OVER DAY CHANGE
Close(Day1) vs Close(Day0)
Did price end higher or lower than yesterday?

Answers: did buyers or sellers win the week?
CLOSE POSITION
(Close − Low) ÷ (High − Low)
Where did price close within today's range?

Answers: who controlled today's session?
EXAMPLE — PRICE ROSE BUT SELLERS WON THE DAY
Day 0 close: ₹280
Day 1: Open ₹285 · High ₹300 · Low ₹283 · Close ₹286
Day-over-day: +₹6 (price rose ✓)
close_pos: (286−283)÷(300−283) = 3÷17 = 0.18 (sellers dominated intraday ✗)
THE COMPLETE PICTURE — VOLUME × CLOSE POSITION
Volume tells you whether a real contest happened. Close position tells you who won it. Together:
HIGH VOL + HIGH CLOSE_POS
Real competition happened. Buyers won. Supply absorbed. Most durable rise. This is VPB — BRK✓.
HIGH VOL + LOW CLOSE_POS
Real competition happened. Sellers won. Distribution — supply overwhelming demand. Bearish. This is DIST −2.
LOW VOL + HIGH CLOSE_POS
No real competition. Sellers stepped away — price drifted up into a vacuum. Fragile. Sellers didn't lose, they retreated. One seller returning reverses it.
LOW VOL + LOW CLOSE_POS
No real competition. Buyers stepped away — price drifted down into a vacuum. Fragile decline. Buyers didn't lose, they retreated. One buyer returning reverses it.
CANDLESTICK PATTERNS ARE JUST NAMED CLOSE_POS OBSERVATIONS
Every candlestick pattern you have ever seen is describing close_pos in plain language. VPB adds volume to make the verdict more reliable.
MARUBOZU
close_pos ≈ 1.0
Buyers all session
HAMMER
close_pos ≈ 0.85
Sellers tried, buyers won
SHOOTING STAR
close_pos ≈ 0.15
Buyers tried, sellers won
CLOSE_POS IS A ONE-DAY VERDICT — NOT A MULTI-DAY FORECAST
WHAT IT TELLS YOU
Who won today's intraday battle
Whether today's volume was absorbed by buyers or sellers
Quality of a breakout or reversal candle
WHAT IT DOES NOT TELL YOU
What happens tomorrow
Whether sellers have more supply remaining
Whether the trend continues or reverses
A low close_pos day can be followed by a strong rally — all the sellers exhausted themselves pushing it down. This is the Wyckoff shakeout. The low close_pos cleared the weak supply. The rise comes the next day when no sellers remain. Close_pos only becomes a sustained signal when sellers keep returning day after day with volume — that is distribution, not a single day's battle.
CHAPTER 4
VPB · Coiling · Breakout
Three concepts that work together to identify the moment when a stock transitions from accumulation to a directional move. Each on its own is incomplete. Together they form the most reliable entry signal in this scanner.
PART 1 — COILING
WHAT IS COILING?
Two conditions must be true simultaneously:

1. Price range tightening — last 5 days' high-low range is less than 4% wide. Each day's swing is smaller than the last.

2. Volume shrinking — last 3 days' volume is below 85% of the 20-day average. Fewer participants each day.

Both conditions together mean: buyers and sellers are reaching temporary agreement. Neither side is willing to push hard. The stock is compressing.
WHAT IS HAPPENING INSIDE A COILING STOCK?
Sellers who were willing to sell at ₹285–₹291 have already sold. Their shares changed hands during the quiet days. Those sellers are gone.

The remaining sellers are sitting at ₹295, ₹300, ₹310. They want more. They are not offering at current price.

Buyers are waiting for evidence the stock will move. They have cash. They have patience. They don't chase.

The order book between current price and those higher sellers is thinning out — becoming sparse. Each day of coiling removes another layer of nearby supply.

This is Scenario 2 from Chapter 1 — sellers disappearing. Not from a sudden event, but gradually. The stock is a spring being compressed.
PART 2 — VPB (VOLUME-PRICE BREAKOUT)
WHY DO BUYERS SUDDENLY COME IN AFTER COILING?
Coiling does not cause VPB. Coiling creates the conditions for VPB. VPB requires high volume — which means both buyers AND sellers must transact. Sellers simply drying up produces a low-volume drift (suspect, not VPB). The actual trigger must bring in new participants:
NEW BUYERS ARRIVE WITH SIZE
VPB requires high volume — which means both buyers AND sellers transacting. New large buyers enter and hit every ask. Sellers fill them but get exhausted. Price closes near the high because buyers absorbed all supply available that day.
EXTERNAL PUSH
Sector news, FII activity, index move. A small external force on a compressed spring causes an amplified release — far beyond what the catalyst alone would cause.
WYCKOFF SHAKEOUT
Brief dip below support flushes weak holders. They sell their shares to strong buyers. No weak hands remain. Stock reverses upward violently — the VPB. The shakeout cleared the final supply.
SMART MONEY
Institutions bought quietly during coiling (hence low volume). When they've accumulated enough they push price with size. The coiling was their accumulation. The VPB is them going for the breakout.
VPB SIGNAL TIERS — WITH ACTUAL NUMBERS
Stock: 20-day avg vol = 5 lakh shares. Price = ₹280. Coiling for 4 days (range 1.8%, vol 72% of avg).
BRK✓ +10 (STRONGEST)
Vol ≥ 2.0× avg = ≥10L shares. Close position ≥ 70% of candle range.
Example: Vol = 11.2L, Open ₹280, High ₹298, Low ₹279, Close ₹294. close_pos = (294−279)/(298−279) = 79%. Buyers dominant all day. Sellers completely overwhelmed.
BRK✓ +7 (STRONG)
Vol ≥ 1.5× avg = ≥7.5L shares. Close position ≥ 60%.
Example: Vol = 8.1L, Close ₹291 out of range ₹280–₹297. close_pos = (291−280)/(297−280) = 65%. Good conviction but slightly weaker than BRK✓.
BRK~ +5 (WEAK BREAKOUT)
Vol ≥ 1.5× avg but close_pos < 60%. Volume came but buyers couldn't hold the highs.
Example: Vol = 8.1L, High ₹297, Close ₹285. close_pos = (285−280)/(297−280) = 29%. Sellers pushed back in the afternoon. Weak signal — wait for confirmation.
DIST −2 (DISTRIBUTION)
High volume but close near the LOW. Price went up intraday then reversed hard.
Example: Vol = 10L, High ₹295, Close ₹281. close_pos = 5%. Sellers came in massive volume on the rally and crushed it. This is supply overwhelming demand — bearish, not bullish.
PART 3 — BREAKOUT: VPB + S/R TOGETHER
CASE 1 — VPB BELOW RESISTANCE
VPB fired but resistance is still above. Stock broke out of coiling but hasn't tested the wall yet. Buyers are aggressive NOW but we don't know if they have enough force to overcome the sellers waiting above at ₹300 (●●●).
CASE 2 — VPB FIRES AT RESISTANCE (ABSORPTION)
The VPB candle itself reaches ₹300 — the resistance. High volume means buyers and sellers met there. If price closes AT or ABOVE ₹300 — buyers absorbed every share sellers offered. The supply at ₹300 is now consumed. Resistance flips to support. Next target is the level above.
CASE 3 — VPB FROM SUPPORT (BASE BREAKOUT)
Stock coiled AT a support level. Buyers defended ₹280 (●●●●) — tested it 4 times. Volume shrank as sellers gave up pushing lower. Then VPB fires upward. The same buyers who defended ₹280 are now going on offence. Support is proven during this trade. Your stop just below ₹280 is anchored to a real structural floor.
CASE 4 — VPB WITH NO STRUCTURE (WEAKEST)
Volume spiked. Price moved. But no support below and no resistance being broken above. The stock is floating between S and R with no context. Could be news, could be momentum, could reverse immediately. You have no structural reference to validate the move or set a logical stop.
THE COMPLETE PICTURE — COILING + VPB + S/R TOGETHER
The ideal setup combines all three. With actual numbers:

Win rate formula: wins ÷ (wins + losses). EXPIRED excluded from denominator to avoid penalising patient setups that neither triggered stop nor hit target within 60 days.

Alpha: signal return − NIFTY 50 return over the same holding period. Alpha > 0 means the signal outperformed the index regardless of market direction.

Score tier: based on F+T only (max 52 in phase tabs) — not F+T+S — so the tier reflects fundamental and technical quality independent of which signal fired.

② Why This Matters for Strategy

Lifecycle validates the core lifecycle hypothesis of the scanner: that stocks progress through a predictable sequence (COIL → BRK → PRE-X → POST-X → TREND) before delivering returns. If the path index shows that full-cycle stocks win at 78% vs partial-cycle at 28%, this is strong evidence that entry timing within the lifecycle is the primary predictor of outcome.

It covers all 2535 stocks — not just the ones you traded. This means you see the full distribution of path outcomes, not just survivorship bias from your selected signals. Stocks that went BRK→PULL→NONE would appear in lifecycle even if you never logged them in Stock-Wise.

Long-term, this data answers: should we enter earlier (COILING) or wait for the EMA cross (POST-CROSS)? If full-cycle stocks outperform, waiting for POST-CROSS is justified and BREAKOUT entries should be smaller.

③ Operational & Display Logic

Scope: all 2535 NSE stocks — completely isolated from signals_log.json. Data: data/lifecycle_log.json written by lifecycle_worker.py at EOD.

D0 reset: WIN → new D0 = outcome date. LOSS → new D0 = outcome date. EXPIRED (60 calendar days) → new D0 = day 61. Previous cycle saved to resolved_cycles[].

Path encoding: 1=COIL · 2=BRK · 3=PRE-X · 4=POST-X · 5=PULL · 6=TREND · 0=NONE (excluded). Path = deduped first-visit sequence e.g. "1→2→4→6".

D0 snapshot: 36 fields captured at cycle start (RSI, ADX, EMA signals, VPB score, F/T scores, PE, D/E, mcap, sector, 52W/38W levels, S/R levels, upsidePct). Never updated mid-cycle.

Panels: Panel 1 = Resolved (WIN/LOSS rows). Panel 2 = Expired. Panel 3 = Path Index table (aggregated stats per unique path). Panels start empty and populate as stocks resolve.

Display: Lifecycle tab fetches /api/lifecycle independently — no dependency on evSignals or signal-tier data.
